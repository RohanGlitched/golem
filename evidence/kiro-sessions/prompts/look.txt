Problems seen on the G1 today:
- The robot walks off-screen within seconds (0.5 m/s forward and a static camera).
- The floor is invisible, with no shadow, so the robot floats in a void.
- The clay is flat.
- The aura is a large spiky leaf shape.

Required, in skeleton/src/ui/stage, ghosts and aura:

1. **Camera follow.**
   - The orbit target tracks the hero pelvis xy with critical damping (about 0.5 s). The user's orbit angle and zoom are kept.
   - Summoning ghosts moves the target smoothly to the ghost swarm's mean pelvis xy, and follows it during playback. Ghost poses are in trial coordinates, so draw the swarm at the trial origin and move the camera there.
   - Replays follow the replayed robot.
2. **The floor as a lit studio floor.**
   - A ShaderMaterial plane in --ground, with a soft elliptical pool of lamp light (about 2.5 m radius, about 15–20% brighter at the centre, smooth falloff) that follows the camera target.
   - Very subtle procedural grain (unfired slip), plus distance fog into the background.
   - Real soft shadows: one DirectionalLight from the key direction with castShadow. Use a 2048 shadow map with PCFSoftShadowMap, and fit the shadow camera tightly around the target (about ±1.6 m) so it moves with the target.
   - Robot meshes cast shadows. The floor receives them; combine the shadow factor in the floor shader or use a ShadowMaterial overlay.
   - Ghosts never cast shadows.
3. **Clay that reads as a sculpted object.** Keep the existing shader structure and add:
   - a fresnel rim (pow 3, about 0.22, slightly cool);
   - a soft low sheen (Blinn, shininess about 12, about 0.06);
   - a hemispheric ambient (slightly warm from above, darker from below);
   - a cavity darkening for downward-facing normals;
   - ACES filmic tone mapping with correct sRGB output.
   The robot must read clearly against the dark ground in both themes.
4. **Vignette.** A CSS radial-gradient overlay on the stage only (no post-processing pass), subtle.
5. **The aura, redesigned as a precise instrument.**
   - The measured boundary is a smooth closed curve on the floor around the robot's feet. Its radius r(θ) is the largest held force per direction, mapped linearly to 0.35–1.1 m, circularly smoothed with a Gaussian kernel (σ ≈ 15°) across the 36 bins, and drawn as a thin bright verdigris band (about 2 px) with a faint inner fill.
   - Cinnabar radial ticks mark the directions where breaks happened, at the break-force radius.
   - Faint reference rings at 100 N and 200 N, labelled in B612 Mono as small floor text (canvas texture sprites, not React).
   - The bins with no data are drawn dashed.
   - It must look like a polar plot etched into the floor, not a shape.
6. **Ghost shader on the G1.** Translucent ectoplasm with a stronger fresnel rim, near-invisible interiors, additive in dark and normal blending in light. Check it with 64 ghosts on the G1 (use the studio's Summon on line 1.3) for both look and frame rate.
7. **First frame.** The robot, already lit and standing on its shadow, within 4 s.
8. **Performance.**
   - Keep render-on-demand.
   - Shadows update only when something moves.
   - 60 fps with the G1 hero alone, and at least 45 fps with 64 G1 ghosts on integrated graphics. Measure with the existing rAF probe on the G1 page, and report it.
